Door day — mount the tablet and go live

eRIGHT Sign-In v4.1. Written 21 October 2026 for Alan. Everything below was checked against the live system on the morning of 21 October; nothing is assumed. Total time: about 1½ hours, most of it at the door.

Already working (checked today): the sign-in server is live (v4.1.0, every health check passing). The door tablet (Raspberry Pi 5 in the Argon 10" case) has been running for 20 hours showing the sign-in page, at network address 192.168.101.54, with its touch screen and on-screen keyboard working. Yesterday staff names were tapped in and out on it and every tap reached the register. It comes back on its own after a restart, and if the server is unreachable when it starts it keeps retrying and shows the page by itself as soon as the server answers (tested on the tablet today: about 30 seconds after the server came back).

You will need

  • The tablet (as it is on your desk now), the USB-203 card reader, the cards and fobs you have, the case's fixings.
  • A network socket by the main door that is on the same office network as the tablet now and gives PoE+ power (the case is powered through its Argon PoE+ HAT — there is no separate power supply), and a network cable long enough.
  • This PC (it can reach the tablet and the server), the Admin PIN (ssh root@192.168.101.102 grep ^ADMIN_PIN /etc/eright-signin.env — never put it in a chat or e-mail).

Part 1 — At your desk (15 minutes)

  1. Prove the card reader. It has not been tried on the tablet yet. Plug the USB-203 into a USB port on the tablet's port edge; its light comes on. (The words Card reader: ready at the bottom of the page are always shown — only a tap proves the reader works.)
    • Tap your own card or fob (you are one of the two staff with a card already): the page says Alan Meade signed IN by card. Tap it again: signed OUT.
    • Tap a card nobody has yet: the page says Card … is not assigned to anyone. That proves reader → tablet → server all work.
    If the page says nothing at all when you tap: unplug and replug the reader, tap again. Still nothing — tell me "cards" and I will look at it from here. Staff can still tap their names meanwhile.
  2. Give out cards (6 of the 8 staff have none yet: George Scoley, Kyle Wood, Sam Birch, Sarah De'Loyde, Scott Clewlow, Toby Gladman). For each card you have: tap it on the tablet (it says not assigned) → on this PC open http://192.168.101.102:3000/admin, enter the PIN → on that person's row press Give last tapped card → confirm. Tap the card again: they are signed in by card. Tap again to sign them out.
  3. Visitor labels. The Brother QL-700 plugs into the tablet's USB and prints a stick-on label for every visitor within a few seconds of them pressing Sign in: VISITOR, eRIGHT Ltd, their name, company, who they are visiting, time in, vehicle and badge number (set up and test-printed 21 Oct). Keep the printer's green Editor Lite light off. The visitor screen tells them to take their label. Reprint or check it from this PC: ssh root@192.168.101.54 "sudo -u labels env $(grep -v ^# /etc/eright-labels.env | xargs) /opt/eright-labels/venv/bin/python /opt/eright-labels/label_agent.py reprint 7" (7 = badge number), or just tell me.
  4. Switch the tablet off cleanly before unplugging it (pulling the cable from a running Pi can damage its SD card). On this PC, in PowerShell: 
    ssh root@192.168.101.54 poweroff
    Wait until the screen goes dark or shows its own blue No Signal box (the panel stays lit while it has power), give it another 10 seconds, then unplug the network cable.

Part 2 — At the main door (30–45 minutes)

  1. Choose the spot. Inside the door, where everyone passes on the way in and out; a height everyone can reach comfortably, including a wheelchair user; out of direct sun (glare); within reach of the network socket.
  2. Mount it with the fixings from the case's box (VESA or panel mount). Argon's online guide does not give the VESA hole spacing, so measure the holes on the back of the case if you are using a bracket. Turn the case so the port edge (network + USB) faces the wall or the cable run, not the visitor.
  3. Fit the card reader beside the screen, plugged into the tablet's USB, where people can tap it without reaching across the screen. Put the QL-700 label printer where a visitor can take the label, plugged into the tablet's other USB port and into mains power.
  4. Plug in the network cable. The tablet starts by itself (the PoE+ HAT's light comes on). Within about 2 minutes the sign-in page appears full-screen.
    If the screen stays black, the socket gives no PoE+ power: try another socket or a PoE injector. If the page shows This site can't be reached, the socket is on a different network from the office one: it will fix itself the moment it can reach the server, so move it to the right socket — nothing needs restarting. (The desktop once showed a warning that the supply "is not capable of 5 A": that is normal on PoE and the tablet measured a healthy 4.98 V with no under-voltage.)

Part 4 — The drill that amazes them (5 minutes, at an announced time)

  1. Say in Teams: "2-minute fire DRILL at 3 pm — practice only, do not leave the building."
  2. At 3 pm, on your phone open the roll-call page → Start a DRILL → confirm. Within seconds a FIRE DRILL — ROLL CALL STARTED card appears in Teams listing who is in, and the door tablet shows the red banner.
  3. Ask people to press I'm SAFE (the Teams card's button, or their home-screen icon). Their names turn green on everyone's roll-call page — no refreshing.
  4. Tick anyone left on the roll-call page, with your name in the Your name (marshal) box. When the last one is ticked: green ALL ACCOUNTED FOR card in Teams.
  5. Press END ROLL CALL: a summary card says how long it took. Admin → Roll-call history keeps it for the fire log.
